# 1. Exam Blueprint and Study Plan

## 1.1 What each exam tests

### Foundation, OGEA-101

Tests knowledge and understanding. Official topic allocation:

- Concepts: **8 questions**
- Introduction to the ADM: **14 questions**
- Introduction to ADM Techniques: **6 questions**
- Introduction to Applying the ADM: **4 questions**
- Introduction to Architecture Governance: **3 questions**
- Architecture Content: **5 questions**

Implication: ADM plus techniques account for half the exam. Do not over-invest in obscure details at the expense of phase purpose, inputs/outputs, iteration, requirements, and stakeholder concepts.

### Practitioner, OGEA-102

Tests application and analysis through eight case-study questions. Options are gradient-scored:

- best: 5 points
- second-best: 3 points
- third-best: 1 point
- incorrect: 0 points

The tested areas include EA context, stakeholder management, Phase A, Phases B-D, Phases E-G, Phase H, Requirements Management, and activities supporting ADM work.

## 1.2 Six-week intermediate plan

### Week 1: Structure and vocabulary

- Learn the 10th Edition structure: Fundamental Content plus Series Guides.
- Master core concepts and glossary.
- Recite ADM cycle and Requirements Management placement.
- Complete 40 recall questions.

### Week 2: Preliminary through Phase A

- Architecture Capability, governance, principles, scope, stakeholders.
- Architecture Vision, Statement of Architecture Work, approval and commitment.
- Practice scenario diagnosis: “Are we ready and authorized?”

### Week 3: Phases B, C, D

- Baseline, Target, gap analysis, candidate roadmap components.
- Business, Data, Application, and Technology domains.
- Trace every architecture choice to concerns and requirements.

### Week 4: Phases E, F, G

- Opportunities and Solutions, Transition Architectures, work packages.
- Migration planning, prioritization, value/risk/dependency.
- Implementation Governance, Architecture Contract, conformance reviews.

### Week 5: Phase H, Requirements, techniques

- Change management and change classification.
- Requirements lifecycle and traceability.
- Stakeholders, business scenarios, interoperability, risk, migration planning.
- Two timed Part 2 practice sets.

### Week 6: Consolidation

- Three timed Part 1 mocks.
- Three timed Part 2 sets.
- Review only weaknesses.
- Navigation practice for the integrated reference.
- Final 48-hour light review.

## 1.3 Daily session, 75 minutes

1. 10 min: closed-book recall.
2. 25 min: study one concept cluster.
3. 20 min: questions or a short scenario.
4. 10 min: explain the topic aloud as an architect.
5. 10 min: update error log and flashcards.

## 1.4 Common legitimate candidate observations

These are study patterns, not claims about live exam content:

- Foundation distractors often contain familiar TOGAF words in the wrong relationship.
- Practitioner options may all sound plausible. Rank them by completeness, sequencing, governance, and fit to the stated problem.
- The longest answer is not automatically best.
- Open-book does not remove the need to know where concepts belong. Searching every question wastes time.
- Answers that jump directly to technology or implementation before scope, stakeholders, vision, requirements, and governance are usually weak.
- An answer can mention a valid technique but still be wrong because it is used in the wrong phase or for the wrong purpose.

## 1.5 Exam-time tactics

### Part 1

- Budget about 75 seconds per question, preserving 10 minutes for review.
- Flag wording such as **best**, **primary**, **purpose**, **most appropriate**, and **first**.
- Eliminate category mistakes: deliverable vs artifact, view vs viewpoint, ABB vs SBB.

### Part 2

- Read the final question before rereading the case.
- Identify: current phase, decision owner, primary concern, missing control, constraint.
- Grade each option against: TOGAF sequence, stakeholder fit, requirements, governance, traceability, practicality.
- Use the reference for exact process or terminology, not as a substitute for reasoning.
- Allocate roughly 10 minutes per case, leaving a final review buffer.

## 1.6 Readiness checklist

- [ ] I can draw the ADM from memory.
- [ ] I can state the purpose of each phase in one sentence.
- [ ] I can explain why Requirements Management is central.
- [ ] I can distinguish baseline, target, gap, roadmap, and implementation plan.
- [ ] I can distinguish deliverable, artifact, and building block.
- [ ] I can explain Architecture Governance and Architecture Capability.
- [ ] I can solve scenarios without keyword matching.
