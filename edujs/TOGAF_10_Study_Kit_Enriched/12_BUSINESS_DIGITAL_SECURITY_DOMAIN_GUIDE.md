# 12. Business, Digital, Security, and Domain Guidance

## Business Architecture toolkit

### Business model

Explains how an organization creates, delivers, and captures value. It supports shared understanding, strategic alternatives, innovation, and resource allocation.

### Business capability

A stable ability or capacity the business possesses or exchanges to achieve an outcome. It describes **what**, not how, why, where, or who. Name capabilities as business nouns or compound nouns. Define name, concise description, roles, core processes, business information, and enabling resources.

### Business Capability Model

A complete, logically organized set of current and desired capabilities for the selected scope, commonly visualized as a capability map. Grouping may use strategic, core, and supporting categories. Use heat maps and cross-mapping to organizations, value streams, information, applications, and initiatives.

### Value stream

End-to-end sequence of value-adding stages creating an outcome for a stakeholder. Name with an active verb-noun form. Define initiating stakeholder and expected value. Each stage should have name, description, participants, entrance criteria, exit criteria, and incremental value item.

### Capability/value-stream mapping

Maps the capabilities needed at each value-stream stage. Heat mapping exposes insufficient capability support and helps focus investment.

### Information map

Business-oriented representation of important information concepts and relationships. Listen for nouns, then test whether the business needs to know, store, or manipulate each concept. Map concepts to capabilities, value streams, and organizations.

### Organization map

Shows internal/external organizational units, partners, stakeholder groups, and formal/informal working relationships across the ecosystem. Unlike a classic organization chart, it emphasizes the network of collaboration, not reporting lines.

## Security and risk

Security Architecture is a coherent set of organizational, conceptual, logical, and physical components that maintains managed risk and security. It is cross-cutting across Business, Data, Application, and Technology Architecture and must not be bolted on after design.

**Risk** is the effect of uncertainty on objectives, including positive and negative deviation. Risk assessment weighs opportunity/threat, likelihood, and magnitude. ERM is continuous and includes risk areas, business impact, assessment, register/model, appetite, and treatment. ISM defines security objectives, assigns risk ownership, implements measures, reports status, and handles incidents.

## Digital enterprise strategies

- **Peek-ahead:** provide just enough risk, standard, and practice guidance for the current minimum viable product while anticipating the next organizational context.
- **EA as services:** provide architecture on demand as enabling consultation, especially across teams where coordination and cognitive load are concerns.

### Digital organizational contexts

1. **Individual/Founder:** minimum essential concerns, digital value, infrastructure, agile/continuous delivery, and risk awareness.
2. **Team:** product, work execution, operations, shared models, collaboration, and risk.
3. **Team of Teams:** cross-team communication, culture, coordination, process mechanisms, portfolio interdependency, value, and cost.
4. **Enduring Enterprise:** governance, risk, security, compliance, information management, architecture/portfolio management, third parties, and long-term sustainability.

## Digital Transformation Readiness Assessment

- **Foundational factors:** minimum readiness, such as vision, sponsorship/direction, IT capability, funding/resources, scope/scale, and business rationale.
- **Impact factors:** amplifiers such as adaptable business model, competence, technology maturity, culture, governance/compliance, and implementation approach.
- **Sustaining factors:** long-term institutionalization, including value realization, policy/regulation, and ecosystem conditions.

Progressive logic: if foundational readiness is absent, address it before expecting impact or sustaining factors to succeed.
